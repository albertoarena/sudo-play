# Trademark and brand policy

Sudo Play is free software, released under the [MIT licence](LICENSE). This page
is about the things the licence does not cover: the project name and the brand
around it.

Nothing on this page takes away any right the MIT licence gives you over the code.

## What the MIT licence covers

The MIT licence covers the **source code** of Sudo Play. You may use it, copy it,
change it, merge it into your own work, publish it, and sell it, for free, as long as
you keep the copyright notice and the licence text with it.

## What this policy covers

The MIT licence is a copyright licence. It does not grant any right to use the
project's name or brand. Those are separate, and they are:

- The name **Sudo Play**, when used for music, playlist or developer tooling.
- The tagline **`$ sudo play --mood friday-deploy`**, and the same line with
  another genre or mood, used as a slogan.
- **All the art made for the project**, in [`art/`](art/) and wherever else it
  appears: the app icon, the menu bar mark, logos, cover and social images, in
  every size and colour variant, still or animated. That includes the copies the
  app ships with, such as `public/icon.icns` and the menu bar images.
- The design, layout and written copy of [sudoplay.dev](https://sudoplay.dev).

These identify this project. They are how people know that a thing came from here,
and they are not part of the MIT grant.

The word `sudo` on its own is not covered. It is the name of a Unix command, and
nobody owns it.

## What you can do without asking

You do not need permission for any of this, and you are welcome to it:

- Say that your project uses, supports, works with, or was inspired by Sudo Play.
- Link to the repository or to [sudoplay.dev](https://sudoplay.dev).
- Write articles, reviews, tutorials, videos or talks about Sudo Play, including
  paid ones, and use the name to say what they are about.
- Use the unmodified art from [`art/`](art/), or a screenshot of the app, to
  illustrate an article, a review or a talk about Sudo Play, with a link back to
  the project.
- Contribute a playlist to the catalog and say so, anywhere, under your own name.
- **Fork the repository on GitHub to contribute.** Nothing on this page applies to
  that. See "Contributing" below.
- Fork the code and change it for your own use, as the licence allows.

## What needs permission

Please do not do any of this without asking first:

- Name **your own** app, product, domain, subdomain, social account or company in a
  way that could be read as this project or as an official version of it. That
  includes `sudo-play-*`, `*-sudo-play` and "Sudo Play" followed by another word,
  used as the product's own name. **A GitHub fork made to contribute is not
  covered**: it keeps this project's name, and it should.
- Use the tagline as the slogan of your own product, or in your own marketing.
- Use the app icon, the menu bar mark or any other art from this project as the
  icon, logo, favicon or hero image of your product, or change it and use the
  result. An app icon in particular is how people tell apps apart in the Dock, in
  Finder and in their menu bar.
- Say or imply that your work is official, endorsed, approved, affiliated with, or
  built by the Sudo Play project, when it is not.
- Register a trademark, domain, bundle identifier or app store name containing
  "Sudo Play", or a name close enough to be confused with it.
- Sell merchandise carrying the name, the art or the tagline.

## Rebuilds and reimplementations

You are free to build your own playlist picker, menu bar music app or developer
mood tool. The idea is not owned by anyone, and a good one is welcome.

If you build your own, give it your own name, your own icon and your own look. Do not
launch it as a "Sudo Play clone", do not put "sudo-play" or "sudoplay" in its
address, and do not carry over the tagline or the art. You may of course say, in the
description, that Sudo Play inspired it, and a link back is appreciated.

The reason is not ownership of the idea. It is that a person who installs your app
and thinks it is this one will ask this project for support, will report its bugs
here, and will judge Sudo Play by software that Sudo Play did not write.

## Contributing: nothing here applies

If you are forking the repository to fix a bug, add a feature, add a playlist or open
a pull request, **stop reading. This page is not about you.**

Fork it, keep the name, keep the app id and the icon exactly as they are, push your
branch, open the PR. Your fork is meant to look like Sudo Play, because it is Sudo
Play. That needs no permission from anyone. See [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md).

The section below is about something different: publishing your version as a separate
app that people install instead of this one.

## Publishing your own distribution

This is a hard fork, not a contribution: you have decided to build and distribute
your own version, under your own name, for other people to install. The MIT licence
lets you, and there is nothing wrong with it.

When you do:

- Give the app a different name, and change its bundle identifier
  (`NATIVEPHP_APP_ID`, `com.albertoarena.sudoplay` here) so macOS treats it as a
  separate app.
- Replace the app icon and the menu bar mark with your own.
- Point it at your own catalog, so your users do not depend on this project's.
- Keep the copyright notice and the licence text, as the licence requires.
- Say in the README that it is a fork of Sudo Play, and link back here.
- Do not use the Sudo Play name, art or tagline as the fork's own brand.

**The test is whether someone can install your version by mistake**, thinking it is
this one. A pull request cannot. A DMG with this icon, or an app store listing with
this name, can.
